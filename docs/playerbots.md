# Playerbots

## Mental model

A playerbot is a normal server `Player` whose decisions come from a scheduled controller instead of a connected client. Each registered bot is loaded from the database, placed in the world, and acted through normal movement, combat, speech, item-use, container, trade, and delay rules. Playerbot code must not assume that `Player::client` exists.

The controller observes current state, selects a goal at a safe boundary, executes one prioritized command per scheduler turn, verifies the result, and reevaluates. Survival and pending irreversible actions take precedence over discretionary work. Decisions are deterministic; language models are not in the control path.

The hunt runtime owns each planning pass, including its [route-selection session](../server/src/playerbothuntrouteselection.h). The controller supplies world observations and executes route queries; the session owns candidate progression, depot and supplier validation, and selection. An adjacent visible monster attacking the bot can interrupt region selection for defensive combat; the bot discards that pass and replans after the threat ends. Other transit combat restrictions remain in force. Cancelling a planning pass discards its route-selection state without cancelling unrelated navigation or changing that bot's hunt cooldowns.

This design is intended to scale without one thread, graphical client, renderer, UI, or blocking loop per bot. The manager reads the `player_bots` roster at startup and reserves its identities before staggered activation. The normal stack seeds `Bot One` in Carlin and `Bot Two` in Thais as level 8 Knights. Controllers, recovery, and hunt cooldowns are independent; world topology, route caches, and the cooperative planning budget remain shared. This establishes a two-bot runtime, not measured population-scale capacity.

## Capabilities and limits

“Ordinary” means available to the normal autonomous controller. “Focused evidence” describes controlled test coverage, not a fixture-only capability. Those tests do not establish how reliably an ordinary character will reach or sustain the behavior during long-running progression.

| Area | Status and evidence | Current boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal selection | Ordinary | Chooses among survival, service, rewards, spell learning, equipment, magic training, and hunting only at safe boundaries; it is not a general-purpose planner. |
| Hunting | Ordinary; map-derived selection has focused real-map coverage | Scores loaded hostile spawns against equipment, skills, health, supplies, route safety, expected XP, and cash pressure. Each accepted outing can adjust one session-local global potion multiplier, so normal evidence transfers proportionally to every area, including mixed-species areas and new equipment snapshots, while the static model preserves relative area difficulty. The observed area also gets a stronger final local estimate; planning uses that estimate instead of multiplying it by the global correction again. Local cheaper evidence remains bounded by compatible capability progression. Food exposure is reported, but calibration currently pools fed and unfed combat; distinguishing their demand is deferred. Supply observations start at hunt-area arrival. Ending health and mana, including fully depleted mana, are reconciled with level restoration into potion-equivalent demand. Interrupted, dangerous, fatal, potion-exhausted, short, low-exposure, and sub-30%-health outings cannot lower estimates. Higher observed demand applies rapidly. Learned supply cost never certifies combat viability, danger, or route safety. Observations reset with the controller, bounded exploration trials are not implemented, and no long progression soak is established. |
| Combat and healing | Ordinary | Approaches reachable same-floor targets, handles attackers, uses melee, potions, and supported Knight spells. After an attempted step or movement-related route plan fails without positional progress, it can stop and fight one adjacent attacker without chase when the existing combat estimates say the fight is survivable. It prefers an attacker on the failed step, otherwise the easiest safe attacker, and retains that target until movement can resume, the target dies or moves out of reach, safety changes, or the workflow is interrupted. Group tactics and other vocations are not developed. |
| Loot | Ordinary; cargo edge cases have focused evidence | Opens owned corpse containers through normal item use before inspecting contents. It merges stacks, opens carried nested containers normally, uses legal free slots, accepts weight-limited partial stacks, and can replace lower-density saleable cargo. Weight and slots are planned separately; currency may replace heavier low-value vendor loot. Equipment, containers, tools, supplies, currency, and unknown items remain protected. `no_capacity` and `no_slot` skip that item without forcing the hunt to end. Skinning and other secondary corpse actions are unsupported. |
| Navigation | Ordinary; transition and recovery scenarios have focused evidence | Routes across the loaded map using a shared coarse topology, then plans and executes local paths and supported transitions. Supports tools, declared ordinary/level/house doors, floor changes, teleports, and registered NPC travel. Known shovel passages keep one topology identity while their live tile opens or decays closed: an open passage is walked without requiring a shovel, while a closed passage requires and verifies normal shovel use before descent. Door access is resolved from the action that would execute; level and house permission checks remain live, and opening must leave the full tile enterable. Locked, quest, unknown, window, and unannotated action-overridden doors are unsupported. Explicitly blocked transition approaches are excluded so local routing can select another approach without discarding the destination. Exhausting a local search budget does not by itself trigger paid travel, and fare or safety rejection counts toward bounded no-progress recovery. Detailed search and recovery have budgets; unsupported transitions and changing obstacles can still prevent a journey. Successful reloads of scripts, item metadata, or global door data rebuild the static topology; failed action, script, or item reloads invalidate it rather than retaining stale routes. |
| NPC service | Ordinary | Discovers loaded shops, bankers, spell trainers, and registered travel offers; buys supplies, sells known loot, banks, and verifies transactions. Supplies are typed stock (health potions, mana potions, ammunition) with per-kind floors, targets, and learned hunt demand, bought from one cash and capacity budget with floors first; only health potions have nonzero targets today. Custom dialogue or opaque travel conditions are excluded unless explicitly modeled. |
| Equipment and depots | Ordinary; restart, remote liquidation, and tool-replenishment paths have focused evidence | Buys or equips supported upgrades, deposits retained loot, and can sell from local or remote depots. A managed Knight or Paladin without a legal weapon or body armor buys the cheapest offer that fills the gap from a discovered shop; it stops with `combat_readiness_missing_*` only when no affordable, reachable offer exists. Paladins use a one-handed throwing weapon (spear family) beside a shield. Weapon comparisons use expected damage, including the loaded distance hit chance; a weapon that breaks more often is a trade-off, not an upgrade. Bow and crossbow loadouts are modeled for readiness but never acquired ([#255](https://github.com/adrunkhuman/tibia-playerbots-project/issues/255)). A managed bot with an empty back slot can buy a standard backpack from a discovered shop. Once that backpack is equipped, it deep-searches carried containers and buys one missing rope (2120), then one missing shovel (2554), from discovered shops only. Each tool purchase requires a free backpack slot, carrying capacity, the normal recovery reserve and route-risk budget, and an exact shop receipt; it does not buy a duplicate found in a nested bag. To upgrade a basic bag, it stages the complete bag in a discovered depot, buys with the empty back slot as the verified receipt destination, then retrieves the old bag into the new backpack. The bot stages only after its bank balance can pay the backpack price and preserve the configured reserve; carried coins inside the bag do not count for this preflight. Bounded failures restore the bag before purchase when possible. An unresolved depot recovery is stored on the player and tagged bag, stops the controller with an explicit failure, and resumes before normal goals after reload. If death equips a replacement basic bag, recovery deposits that complete bag before restoring the tagged original. Tool replacement uses this ordinary equipment workflow and has no separate death-recovery path. Other containers and two-handed loadout trade-offs are unsupported and protected. |
| Spells | Ordinary for audited Knight spells; calibration and overflow training have focused evidence | Can learn and use selected healing, support, and offensive spells through normal speech. Loaded spell rules remain authoritative. Observations may rank legal casts but never weaken safety or legality. |
| Rewards and Oracle | Ordinary goal selection; claiming and departure have focused evidence | Claims supported shared container rewards and one legacy doublet case. The tagged Oracle departure path supports a level 8–10 character with no vocation. Scripted quests, levers, hazardous quest transit, and general dialogue reasoning are unsupported. |
| Death and restart | Ordinary; failure paths have focused evidence | Uses normal death, save, temple login, and bounded relog recovery. It reconstructs intent from persisted state rather than resuming an interrupted route, conversation, open container, or transaction in place. |
| Social behavior | Planned | The two bots act independently. Parties, guilds, trading between players, relationships, personalities, and generated login rhythms are not implemented. |
| Speech | Planned | No language-model dialogue or personality voice exists. |

At hunt selection, total carried and bank gold below the cash buffer enables coin-income preference after safety and supply fit, with XP as the tie-breaker. At or above the buffer, normal XP ranking applies. This does not interrupt an active hunt or change purchases. If no coin-producing hunt fits the constraints, selection keeps its existing fallback and reports `coin_income_fallback` alongside route rejection counts. Coin income excludes saleable loot and does not claim net profit after supply or travel costs.

XP calibration applies a bounded observed-to-predicted correction locally and to untested areas. Its range allows substantial underestimation to be corrected beyond the former twofold ceiling; it does not relax combat, supply, or route safety checks.

Supply calibration is deliberately asymmetric. One accepted observation updates the policy-global multiplier once, regardless of how many atlas variants planning later scores. Safe downward evidence blends at `0.10` and cannot reduce the multiplier below `0.25`; this transfers useful evidence without letting easy hunts erase the static model. The observed area's final local estimate blends at `0.20`, so it adapts faster and decays toward zero naturally without a sample-count snap. Higher demand raises both applicable estimates immediately. Local estimates are absolute final rates and override the global estimate, which prevents double application. These constants affect supply preference only; static threat, lethal-risk, challenge, emergency reserve, and route checks remain authoritative.

Focused scenarios often use fixed destinations, controlled monsters, teleports, or seeded state. Dynamic hunt, route, and target-approach scenarios exercise loaded-world planning, but none proves unattended progression, stable economics, or hundreds-of-bots performance. Plans and temporary gaps belong in the [issue tracker](https://github.com/adrunkhuman/tibia-playerbots-project/issues), not this document.

Navigation is hierarchical: [`PlayerBotTopology`](../server/src/playerbottopology.cpp) indexes the loaded map into connected local regions and transition edges. The [controller](../server/src/playerbotcontroller.cpp) selects a global route, refines its next segment into tile-level actions, and considers eligible NPC travel connections. Whole-map routing describes its scope, not a guarantee that every destination is reachable.

Hunt selection uses a [resumable route planner](../server/src/playerbothunttravel.cpp). Walking and NPC approach connections retain a coarse itinerary through connected pieces of the map's sectors. Ordinary segments use destination-guided tile search; stairs and teleports remain explicit crossings, checked against the live world. An unsafe or failed itinerary first tries an alternative coarse route, then searches progressively wider corridors of connected map pieces before falling back to the whole live graph. An itinerary whose coarse danger estimate and walked prefix both exceed the risk budget skips those fallbacks and stays unknown. All attempts share the original connection allowance; a restricted failure cannot prove unreachability. When the source and every goal are graph nodes, a graph "no path" is final for the loaded map; off-graph endpoints retain live validation. The graph includes every possible rope and ladder landing for connectivity, but itineraries use only the landing the climb script chooses. It also includes traversable doors, known shovel passages, and tiles blocked only by movable items, but not parcel-only climbs or machete/pick passages. Live tile changes still invalidate affected detailed searches. Scripted or admin map edits that add routes require `/reload scripts` after temporary blockers clear; the rebuild uses the live map. Corridor and whole-map searches use a shortcut-aware lower bound that includes directed teleports and floor redirects. Its relaxed geometry ignores obstacles and collapses floors, so guidance remains optimistic rather than excluding potentially better routes. Live shortcut changes restart affected search priorities; ordinary map changes do not discard the guidance. All valid adjacent NPC approach tiles form one destination set rather than separate searches; teleport tiles are never approach goals. Coarse travel estimates cannot establish an accepted route or reject one for danger: detailed connections supply that evidence. Each bot owns a risk profile (currently the same defaults). Hunt and service route decisions use detailed risk evidence; a required hunt return can use the least-risk executable depot approach when no safe one is available. The selector retains alternative fare and risk labels and uses safe route-cost bounds rather than a fixed shortlist. Paid offers must leave the minimum recovery reserve; free offers remain eligible.

Depot liquidation keeps seller candidates and unfinished routes across turns. Before detailed planning, it rules out candidates with no affordable travel chain or possible profit, using lower bounds on fare and travel time. It checks the remaining candidates in order of potential profit, then revalidates routes before withdrawing stock. A failed source route rules out that depot's other sellers for the scan. A failed seller route tries other conversation tiles before rejecting the seller. Service approaches track a wandering NPC, wait briefly if it leaves speech range, and replan around blocked steps. Withdrawals respect backpack space and carrying capacity; excess stock waits for another trip. Paid travel retains the recovery fare reserve. Newly available travel offers may wait until the next scan. Candidate snapshots and final paid-route confirmation still run synchronously.

Coarse connectivity, successful ordinary walking segments and combat-profile-specific danger samples use bounded, shared caches. Live movement checks and watched map/line-of-sight dependencies protect reuse across callers; capability, risk-model and topology inputs remain part of cache identity. Unrestricted fallbacks reuse a source search tree across goals within the same actor's planning pass. Retargeting changes frontier priorities while retaining final settled costs. A settled goal supplies a route once the frontier lower bound rules out a cheaper target; otherwise the existing search continues. Unsettled goals remain unknown at the limit, never proof of unreachability or an unreachable-region cooldown. A graph-connected path that exhausts the detailed-search allowance remains unknown. Relevant world and provider changes invalidate dependent work. The [shared planning budget](../server/src/playerbotplanningbudget.h) charges actual elapsed work across controllers and queues continuations fairly. It does not create parallel game threads or preempt an expensive operation. Topology primitives and candidate output remain synchronous, so neither the budget nor focused fixtures establish a hard latency bound or measured ten-bot capacity. Movement and NPC execution still replan against the live world.

Hunt departure reuses the selected outbound plan when its origin, actor facts, risk, world dependencies, and required outbound/depot/supplier proofs remain valid. Paid-leg proofs also check travel offers and provider anchors. Stale evidence falls back to the bounded route planner; normal movement checks and later-leg replanning still apply. Departure validates a return route and protects its fare. An incomplete patrol preflight has a separate three-attempt budget, with 1- and 2-second retry delays. Exhausting that budget returns for service with `hunt_region_planning_incomplete`, without removing waypoints or applying an unreachable-region cooldown.

Depot discovery checks the validated hunt exit before scanning other lockers; liquidation sources remain subject to the normal risk limit. Within a depot, occupied approaches rank last. If its selected approach is occupied nearby or navigation stalls, the bot scans for another locker; a creature that stalls the walk is first waited out within the shared approach bound; a remote liquidation trip waits for its validated source approach. Exact NPC interaction, depot, and spawn patrol approaches exclude floor-change and teleport tiles: passing through a transition does not mean the bot can finish standing there. Depot route validation checks this again against the live tile. These endpoint rules do not prohibit intentional transition traversal. Patrols reuse return-route evidence across complete, same-floor walking paths with no modeled danger; added danger, transitions, route failures, or changed route inputs require revalidation. Rejected patrol plans enter bounded recovery rather than retrying indefinitely. On the way to the hunt area, blocked steps do not end the hunt: the bot retries the leg until it has reached no new tile for 45 seconds of navigation; time spent fighting or looting does not count. An adjacent monster that attacks while it blocks the route is fought through the existing route-critical defense; other blockers are waited out. Inside the hunt area, the bot passes over up to a quarter of the circuit's waypoints per hunt without removing them; the next failure ends the region with a 10-minute cooldown. A patrol never avoids its own waypoint because the final step to it failed. A validated paid route boards the offer it validated, using the route engine's own walk to that captain; a captain that wandered since planning is followed to its live tiles within the shared approach bound. After a waypoint's trip is validated, later legs toward it, including legs after traversal combat or around a blocked tile, use the ordinary planner under the same risk checks. A leg may board only the validated itinerary's boats, within its fare, while the cached return validation still holds; anything else, or 32 legs, revalidates the whole trip. Depot-bound travel needs enough money for the trip, not for a later potion restock; route safety rules still apply. Supplier travel after reaching the depot retains the restock reserve.

## Knowledge and game boundaries

Bots may use static facts a player could learn and remember: map geography, known spawn areas, quest locations, and loaded NPC services. They must not read hidden live state to bypass game mechanics. In particular, a bot identifies corpses through normal container and ownership metadata, opens them normally, and only then inspects contents.

The world is not observer-gated: bot behavior does not become cheaper or less real when no human is nearby. Normal engine legality, action delays, path execution, payment, inventory capacity, spell costs, cooldowns, danger checks, and item verification remain authoritative. Focused fixtures may arrange state directly, but fixture coordinates and shortcuts must not enter production knowledge.

## Lifecycle and persistence

A controller is `Running`, `Paused`, or `Stopped`. Running controllers schedule bounded turns. Paused is a fixture checkpoint that suppresses turns without ending the controller. Stopped is terminal: pending work is cancelled, one terminal event is emitted, and that controller must never schedule or emit another turn.

Player data, inventory, equipment, learned spells, storages, and depot contents persist through the normal player save path. Routes, targets, conversations, open containers, pending actions, hunt observations, and objective state are memory-only and are reevaluated after relog or restart. Death retains server ownership while bounded recovery reloads the same character. A human cannot log into a reserved bot, including during delayed activation, recovery, startup failure, or after terminal removal. The manager retains each reservation until the process exits. Ordinary removal terminates only that bot; clean shutdown terminates all controllers before normal player saving. Roster changes take effect on restart.

The `playerbot-setup` Compose service provisions both development accounts, characters, and `player_bots` registrations before the server. It is idempotent and fails on SQL errors rather than taking over an unrelated same-named or deleted character. The single-bot baseline is [`insertPlayerbots.sql`](../server/schema/insertPlayerbots.sql); [`insertBotTwo.sql`](../server/schema/insertBotTwo.sql) adds the second Knight. Existing gameplay and connectionless overlays provision only Bot One and require a single-bot database. Setup may refill empty equipment slots; persistence checks must restart only the server with `--no-deps`.

## Telemetry contract

Playerbot telemetry is JSON Lines on server stdout. Every record has this stable envelope:

- `schema: 1`
- UTC RFC 3339 `ts`
- `component: "playerbot"`
- `server_run_id`, which correlates all playerbot records from one server process
- monotonic server-run `sequence`
- `controller_id`, which identifies a controller incarnation within `server_run_id` and changes on recovery; manager-only lifecycle records use `null` and identify the related controller separately
- `event`, `bot`, persistent `player_id`, and `position`

Manager lifecycle records identify the affected registered bot and use `related_controller_id` for its preceding controller when available. Failed roster identities may use `player_id: 0`; a failure for one identity does not prevent another bot from starting.

Event-specific fields are conditional. Machine-readable states, actions, results, statuses, and reasons use lowercase values. The main families cover lifecycle and goals, action verification, navigation and NPC travel, hunting and supplies, rewards and equipment, spells, liquidation, and periodic operational summaries. Summaries include `player_state_available`, the current goal, phase, activity, target, inexpensive character resources, and applicable waiting or recovery context. Resource fields are `null` unless the controller can resolve its expected server-owned player identity. Successful movement is not emitted once per tile, but emitted JSONL events are not repeat-suppressed. Completed hunt planning emits every scored candidate once per `planning_pass` and `scoring_revision`; later candidate records carry `candidate_phase: "route_validation"` only when route validation changes that candidate. Hunt candidates report `static`, `global`, or `local` supply estimate sources, the reason, the global multiplier and sample count, the static and applied rates, and any local-capability rejection. Every `hunt_region_outcome` reports observation acceptance and reason, separate global and local update directions, global multiplier and sample counts before and after, capability changes, exposure guards, resource debt, and the resulting local sample count. Selection, failure, cancellation, and stale-revision records carry the same planning attribution. Telemetry does not apply a separate candidate cap, while planner and per-turn work budgets still apply normally. A controller's `terminal` event is final; later manager lifecycle records are outside that terminated controller stream.

```powershell
docker compose -f server/compose.yaml logs --no-log-prefix --since 30m server | Where-Object { $_ -match '"component":"playerbot"' }
```

JSONL is authoritative and is unaffected by in-game announcement settings. Docker still rotates server logs at the configured retention limit (ten 10 MiB files); capture the stream to an artifact when a development run needs longer-lived evidence. By default, concise playerbot intent and outcome messages go to every online player's orange console feed, without a center-screen announcement. Any character can send the managed bot the exact private message `logs off` or `logs on` to disable or enable only that bot's announcements. The bot confirms the setting privately. The setting survives that bot's death and controller reconstruction, but resets to enabled when the server restarts. Human announcements have separate short-window deduplication.

Producers in [`playerbot.cpp`](../server/src/playerbot.cpp) and [`playerbottelemetry.cpp`](../server/src/playerbottelemetry.cpp), plus the assertions under [`scripts/playerbot-gameplay/`](../scripts/playerbot-gameplay/), own event detail. Consumers must tolerate additional event-specific fields rather than depending on human-readable logs or per-step output.
